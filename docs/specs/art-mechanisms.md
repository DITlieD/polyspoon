# Mechanical construction art probes

## Before-capture scope

The original stone well and wheelbarrow are captured with the existing recipe renderer before production edits. Each packet includes neutral front, rear, left, right, top, oblique and rear-oblique views plus all existing presets. The manifests bind parameters, camera/stage, browser renderer, image digests and before/after source identity. No external meshes, images or vendor output form part of these fixtures.

## Wheelbarrow silhouette and construction

Category: an open rural carrying barrow. Its identifying cues are a flared plank tray, one deliberate wheel and long separate carrying handles. The tray is the primary mass. The wheel and varied-weight underframe support it. The dominant direction runs from the rear grips toward the lower front nose. Preserve the open tray and the air between handles, tray and ground. It must not resemble a tall storage crate placed on a wheel.

Use the existing width/length/depth controls as the tray's outer upper envelope and wall height. Author a narrower lower footprint and outward wall planes, with a lower/sloped front profile. Board courses follow those planes rather than stacking unrelated horizontal boxes. Give the floor, wall courses and rim distinct thicknesses. Bearers and risers visibly connect the tray to the carrying frame; chassis members carry different loads and need different weights.

Retain solid and spoked wheel choices. Establish dark tread, inset wooden face/rim, smaller hub and subordinate braces/spokes as a hierarchy. Preserve wheel-axle pivot position, fixed axle and supports, existing semantic IDs, role colors and independent material ownership. No new control or shared helper is proposed for the first candidate.

## Well silhouette and construction

Category: a masonry village well with an open shaft and supported timber shelter. Its cues are broad staggered masonry, a purposeful coping lip and a visible winding mechanism between substantial posts. The shaft/coping are the primary lower mass; the roof is one coherent upper mass. Preserve the central shaft opening, clearance around the crank and bucket/rope path, and air under the roof. It must not read as stacked identical cylinder bands beneath equally spaced roof stripes.

Replace uniform masonry repetition with designed row boundaries and staggered joints. Use broad controlled block facets and restrained coherent silhouette variation. Coping forms a deliberate supporting edge rather than another interchangeable course. Keep the shaft open and preserve credible wall thickness.

Author roof section overlap and readable eave thickness using a small number of coherent roof planes/board groups. Keep broad roof color areas calm; avoid black seams between every strip. Align roof, ridge, rafters, braces and posts so the load path is visible. Retain existing roof/mechanism/bucket toggles, diameter/height/roof-size/drop controls and their relationships. Preserve the windlass pivot and fixed bearings, supports, rope and bucket. No physics or winding behavior is introduced.

## Inputs, errors and compatibility

Existing control keys, ranges, numeric units, color roles and dependency failures remain authoritative. Invalid dimensions, unknown fields and a bucket without the windlass must continue to reject. Extrema must keep finite grounded geometry, open interiors, connected supports and valid semantic references. Changes to construction are intentional art candidates; version/migration and delivery work follow design review. No current public package or verified production preview is regenerated in this phase.

## Affected callers and tests after release

The two owned recipe builders feed the existing registry, canonical build adapter, browser, CLI/MCP, procedural module and GLB exporter. Their local construction functions and returned anchors/joint declarations are the direct change surface. Shared geometry helpers, semantic core, registration, configuration and migrations remain unchanged.

Focused tests will cover flared tray station relationships and clear opening, floor/rim/course thickness, axle/support invariants under dimensions and wheel styles, staggered well row/coping construction, roof support/overlap, mechanism/bucket toggles and existing negative dependencies. Test results establish construction behavior only. First-candidate source edits stop before coordinated diagnostic capture and individual visual review.

## Well repair after first-candidate review

The first candidate retained a precision-ring read and oversized sheet-like roof sections. Its source, diff, inputs and images remain in the mechanical evidence packet.

The repair uses individual convex masonry footprints with broad front/side planes and substantial bevel stations. Course boundaries vary coherently around the shaft, with adjacent rows sampling the same authored boundary function. Stone bodies overlap the mortar core; the shaft and coping envelope remain clear. Variation changes physical dimensions, not per-face colors.

The roof has two overlapping courses per slope, divided into cover pieces around 0.42 m wide. Controlled width, end and lap-height variation explains separate wooden pieces while preserving quiet color areas. A single ridge cover conceals the meeting cover edges. Existing post, roof, mechanism and bucket controls keep their relationships.

Roof speckle diagnosis uses the unchanged stage and rejected geometry. Shadow receiving/casting and global shadow removal produce no meaningful change across the sampled roof surface. A geometric probe found no coincident top hits, with the closest sampled surfaces about 9.59 mm apart. Explicit normals and tiny per-mesh depth offsets did not explain the broad pattern. Its precise remaining cause is unresolved; no lighting, shared renderer, shared helper or material-palette workaround was applied. The repaired geometry requires a fresh same-stage capture before judging that artifact.
